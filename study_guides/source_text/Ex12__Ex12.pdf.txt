    Exercise 12: UART Receiver
    • We will implement the UART receiver to work with our transmitter
       •   8N1 frame format
       •   9600 Baud rate

    • UART receiver monitors for falling edge on the line → this is the start bit
    • Once this is detected, we want to wait half a Baud period before sampling
      the value on the line (sample in the middle of the period)
    • From then on, we are sampling every Baud period for subsequent bits

    • Use a 9-bit shift register for this design.
       • Refer back to the UART TX design if you forget why 9 bits and why shift register.
    • It should shift a total of 10 times, and the start bit should “fall off” the end
      of the 9-bit shift register. Bits [7:0] of the shift register are the received data.

    • Follow the same process you use for designing the transmitter

    • You will need:
       • Shift registers to hold data received
       • Counter for baud rate – sets up when to sample data
       • Counter for which bit we are at
       • FSM to manage control signals – when to do what
       • Synchronizer to handle metastability issue for RX

    • Many components needed are similar to ones in TX. You can take your
      TX design and modify parts of it.
